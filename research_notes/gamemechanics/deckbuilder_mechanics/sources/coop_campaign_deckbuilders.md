# Co-operative, Solo, Campaign and Legacy Deck-Builders: Mechanics Catalogue

Scope note: mechanics are named generically (bold), explained in 1–3 sentences, and attributed to games. Facts carry inline sources. Items drawn from background knowledge that could not be verified this session are kept out of Cited Findings and listed under Gaps as "[unverified]" so the report writer can choose whether to verify or drop them. Sources are mostly rulebook mirrors (rulespal, fan wikis that transcribe rulebooks), publisher news posts and reviews; BoardGameGeek pages were not fetched directly.

Games checked and found **not to be deck-builders** (excluded or only noted): Ghostbusters: The Board Game (Cryptozoic) is dice-and-miniatures scenario play ([Blogcritics](https://blogcritics.org/board-game-review-ghostbusters-from-cryptozoic/)); The Elder Scrolls: Betrayal of the Second Era is a dice/character-mat adventure game with a three-session campaign, not a deck-builder ([Punchboard](https://punchboard.co.uk/the-elder-scrolls-betrayal-of-the-second-era-review/), [Board Game Quest](https://www.boardgamequest.com/the-elder-scrolls-betrayal-of-the-second-era-review/)). Hostage Negotiator is a "hand-builder" variant (included). Arkham Horror LCG is deck *construction*, not in-game deck-building (included as contrast).

## 1. Automated threats: how the game becomes the opponent

### Takeaway
Co-op deck-builders replace a human opponent with one or more **pre-structured enemy decks** (nemesis/villain/invader/hive/terror decks) whose order is partly authored (tiers, stages) and partly random, plus **timers** (turn-order decks, Master Strikes, scheme twists, power countdowns) that make the threat escalate without any decision-making AI. The most elegant systems telegraph the next threat (Spirit Island's visible invader track, Aeon's End power tokens, Slay the Spire intents) so players can plan against it.

### Cited Findings

**Tiered threat deck (authored escalation)**
- **Tiered nemesis deck**: the enemy deck is built from three tiers stacked Tier 3 at the bottom, Tier 2 in the middle, Tier 1 on top, and is never shuffled after building, so the threat strengthens on a known schedule; basic cards per tier scale with player count (Tier 1: 1/3/5/8 basics for 1–4 players; Tier 3: 7 at all counts) and some nemeses add a Tier 0. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki: Rules](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Staged invader deck**: 12 cards, 3 Stage I on top, 4 Stage II in the middle, 5 Stage III at the bottom; Stage II cards bearing a flag trigger the adversary's escalation effect. — Spirit Island — [Rulespal: Spirit Island rulebook](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Deck-exhaustion as clock (both directions)**: in Aeon's End, players win if the nemesis deck is empty and no minions/powers remain in play, and if the nemesis must draw from an empty deck it instead Unleashes three times; in Spirit Island, players **lose** if they must draw an invader card and the deck is empty. — Aeon's End, Spirit Island — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules); [Rulespal: Spirit Island](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Enemy card taxonomy (Aeon's End nemesis deck)**
- **Instant attack cards**: resolve immediately, then discard. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Persistent minions**: enter play with life (and optionally shield) tokens and fire a PERSISTENT effect every nemesis turn until killed; shields absorb one source of damage each regardless of amount. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Countdown powers**: enter with X power tokens, lose one each nemesis turn, and fire a large effect when the last is removed; many have a "TO DISCARD" cost players can pay on their turn to defuse them. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Silence/skip token**: a silence token on a minion can be removed instead of resolving its persistent effect. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Unleash (nemesis-specific signature effect)**: a per-nemesis effect that generic cards call; it fires extra times on player exhaustion (twice) and empty deck (three times). — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Nemesis-specific resource meters**: individual nemeses track their own build-up, e.g. Rageborne strikes at end of its turn when holding 4+ Fury tokens. — Aeon's End — [search summary citing Aeon's End rule sources](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/aeons-end-complete-rules/)

**Turn-order randomisation**
- **Shuffled turn-order deck**: a small deck of player and nemesis turn cards is drawn one at a time to decide who acts next; when empty it is reshuffled, so the nemesis may act twice in a row or not for a while. Expedition modifiers can swap a nemesis turn card for an "Unleash" turn card. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules); [OfficialGameRules summary](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/aeons-end-complete-rules/)

**Villain row / city conveyor (Legendary family)**
- **Villain conveyor with escape**: villains enter a limited row; when it fills, the oldest is pushed out and "escapes", triggering a punishment (e.g. Escape: each player gains a Wound). — Marvel Legendary — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/legendary-marvel-review/); [BGG card text wiki](https://boardgamegeek.com/wiki/page/Legendary_Marvel_Complete_Card_Text_-_Villains_and_Adversaries)
- **Master Strike (boss bonus action card)**: mixed into the villain deck; when drawn the Mastermind takes a bonus action. — Marvel Legendary — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/legendary-marvel-review/)
- **Scheme Twist (scenario-defined escalation)**: generic twist cards in the villain deck whose effect is defined by the current Scheme card, so one deck structure supports many scenarios; schemes also define loss conditions (e.g. via escapes or wounds). — Marvel Legendary — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/legendary-marvel-review/)
- **Hostage capture**: villains capture bystanders; a villain that escapes with a bystander makes each player discard a card, and defeating it rescues the bystander. — Marvel Legendary — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/legendary-marvel-review/)
- **Hidden-enemy corridor (face-down complex)**: enemies advance face-down through rooms of a "complex" (shown as motion-tracker blips); reaching the combat zone makes them harder to kill. — Legendary Encounters: Alien — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/); [Order of Gamers rules summary](https://www.orderofgamers.com/downloads/LegendaryEncountersAlien_v1.1.pdf)
- **Scanning (pay to reveal)**: spending attack points reveals a face-down card; it may be an event rather than an alien and may drop an alien straight into combat. — Legendary Encounters: Alien — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/)
- **Strike deck (random damage severity)**: enemies in the combat zone make players draw strike cards ranging from flesh wounds to severe injuries. — Legendary Encounters: Alien — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/)
- **Random threat seeding**: random drone cards are mixed into each set of threats, so a full-grown enemy can appear early. — Legendary Encounters: Alien — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/)
- **Objective/scenario chain**: play proceeds through objectives (in Firefly, "episodes" in groups of three, with optional Side Jobs that reward or punish). — Legendary Encounters: Alien, Firefly — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/); [Miniature Market review: Firefly](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/legendary-encounters-firefly-deck-building-game-review)
- **Second front (ship enemies)**: some enemies strike a shared vehicle instead of a player, turn after turn, and losing the ship loses the game. — Legendary Encounters: Firefly — [Miniature Market review](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/legendary-encounters-firefly-deck-building-game-review)

**Event decks and location pressure**
- **Location-scaled event draw**: each turn the active hero reveals a number of Dark Arts event cards set by the current Location, then each villain's ability fires. — Harry Potter: Hogwarts Battle — [Rulespal: Hogwarts Battle](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)
- **Control-token territory loss**: villains add control tokens to the current Location; when it is full, heroes have until end of turn to remove tokens, otherwise the location falls and the next one is revealed; losing all locations loses the game. — Harry Potter: Hogwarts Battle — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)
- **Villain replacement queue**: a defeated villain gives its printed reward and is replaced from the villain stack at end of turn. — Harry Potter: Hogwarts Battle — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)

**Spirit Island's invader AI (see Section 5 for detail)**
- **Sliding action track (telegraphed AI)**: each invader card moves Explore → Build → Ravage over three turns, so players always see which land types will be ravaged next turn and built in this turn. — Spirit Island — [OfficialGameRules](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/spirit-island/); [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Escalating difficulty modules**: optional Adversaries (e.g. Brandenburg-Prussia, England, Sweden) and Scenarios (rated 0–10) change setup and rules; scenarios override adversaries on conflict. — Spirit Island — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Solo timers and AI players**
- **Dummy player as round timer**: in solo Mage Knight a dummy player flips cards each turn; when either the player's or the dummy's deck runs out, the day/night round ends. — Mage Knight — [BoardGaming.com tips](https://boardgaming.com/gametips/mage-knight-board-game); [BGG Mage Knight FAQ](https://boardgamegeek.com/wiki/page/Mage_Knight_FAQ)
- **Terror deck with pivotal finale**: an event deck raises the threat track or offers opportunities (minor demands, hostage releases); its bottom card is a Pivotal Event that pressures the end of the game. — Hostage Negotiator — [Derailments of Thought](https://derailmentsofthought.com/2016/01/17/hostage-negotiator-board-game-review/); [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/hostage-negotiator-review/)
- **Rolled intent (enemy behaviour die)**: the board game replaces the video game's displayed intent with a die roll read against each enemy card's behaviour table; turns run Draw, Play, Enemy Intent, Upkeep. — Slay the Spire: The Board Game — [OfficialGameRules: Slay the Spire](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/slay-the-spire/); [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/slay-the-spire/rulebook) (source quality moderate)
- **Hidden-fate deck (peek at backs)**: each turn players see only card backs of the top three cards and choose one, a lightweight AI where the scenario deck itself is the opponent. — Paleo — [GameRules: Paleo](https://gamerules.com/rules/paleo/)

**Traitor / turned player**
- **Turned player**: a player whose deck receives a chestburster and draws it dies and continues as an alien with a new role and deck; optional agendas can create a traitor. — Legendary Encounters: Alien — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/)

### Inferences
- Two families of automated opponent exist: **authored-order decks** (Aeon's End tiers, Spirit Island stages) where difficulty curve is designed, and **shuffled decks with special cards** (Legendary's Master Strikes and Scheme Twists) where escalation arrives at random but density is controlled. A hospital game could seed "surge" cards into tiers of an arrivals deck.
- Countdown cards with a pay-to-defuse cost (Aeon's End powers) create meaningful triage-style choices: deal with the ticking problem now or invest elsewhere.
- Telegraphing (Spirit Island track, power tokens, Slay the Spire intents) is the common tool for making an AI opponent feel fair.

### Gaps
- Marvel Legendary exact Master Strike/Mastermind "tactic" structure (mastermind has four tactic cards defeated one at a time) and city space names (Sewers, Bank, Rooftops, Streets, Bridge) — [unverified], from background knowledge.
- Legendary Encounters: Alien's Hive deck, avatar cards, Coordinate keyword and HQ row: only partially confirmed (Coordinate and HQ from [Order of Gamers summary](https://www.orderofgamers.com/downloads/LegendaryEncountersAlien_v1.1.pdf) snippets). Predator (hunt/trophies, playing as Predators) not researched.
- Slay the Spire board game co-op details (shared rows, ally-targeted Block) only from a [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/board-games/the-slay-the-spire-board-game-might-be-a-little-too-faithful-to-the-videogame-but-its-co-op-mode-offers-a-whole-new-perspective-on-the-iconic-roguelike); intent die rules not read from the official rulebook.
- Hero Realms boss decks (many-vs-one) exist but their rules were not found ([Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2018/04/11/matt-carlson-hero-realms/)).
- Fantastic Beasts (Perilous Pursuits / Hogwarts Battle-style titles) not researched.

## 2. Shared resources, shared life, defeat conditions and junk cards

### Takeaway
Co-op deck-builders usually pair **individual health** with a **shared thing to protect** (a city, a location sequence, an island, a ship), and losses come from several independent clocks. Enemies pollute decks with **dead cards** (wounds, corruption, status/curse, chestbursters) that clog hands, a direct deck-building analogue of damage.

### Cited Findings
- **Shared defended entity**: Gravehold starts at 30 life (adjustable for difficulty), cannot exceed its start value, and at 0 the players lose. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Exhaustion with spill-over**: players have 10 life; at 0 they are exhausted, the nemesis Unleashes twice, the player destroys a breach and loses charges, cannot heal, and any further damage to them deals double to Gravehold; all players exhausted is a loss (except True Solo). — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Scenario-added loss track**: Legacy of Gravehold adds a loss at 10 fire tokens; individual nemeses add their own loss conditions. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Knock-out without elimination (Stunned)**: a hero at 0 health is Stunned, discards half their hand, adds a villain control token to the location, and resets to 10 health at end of turn. — Harry Potter: Hogwarts Battle — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)
- **Territory as life**: losing all Locations to villain control loses the game. — Harry Potter: Hogwarts Battle — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)
- **Blight pool with flip-side doom**: the Blight card starts Healthy; if its blight supply runs out it flips to Blighted Island (never flips back), usually leading to a loss; adding blight destroys spirit presence and cascades to adjacent land if already blighted. — Spirit Island — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Multiple loss clocks**: Spirit Island loses on blight overrun, any spirit losing all presence, or invader deck exhaustion. — Spirit Island — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Wound cards (dead draws)**: wounds take hand space with no ability and can only be removed by specific effects or by healing (taking no actions). — Marvel Legendary — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/legendary-marvel-review/)
- **Wounds reduce draw size**: heroes start with 6 HP, each wound lowers it, and end-of-turn draw equals current HP; wounds heal at the Village or with potions (the Temple heals two but disables the lantern and levelling). — Thunderstone Quest — [Rulespal / TSQ rulebook summary](https://rulespal.com/thunderstone-quest/rulebook); [Meeple Mountain review](https://www.meeplemountain.com/reviews/thunderstone-quest/)
- **Lethal junk card (time-bomb in deck)**: an unremoved facehugger shuffles a chestburster into the player's deck; drawing it kills the player. — Legendary Encounters: Alien — [Space-Biff!](https://spacebiff.com/2014/10/10/alien-deck-building/)
- **Negative-VP junk (Corruption)**: corruption cards have no type or ability and cost victory points at game end. — LOTR Deck-Building Game (Cryptozoic) — [Rulespal: Fellowship DBG](https://rulespal.com/lotr-fellowship-deck-building/rulebook)
- **Status/curse/daze decks**: the board game includes separate Daze, Status and Curse decks used to pollute player decks. — Slay the Spire: The Board Game — [OfficialGameRules](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/slay-the-spire/)
- **Skull failure track + deck-mill wounds**: unfed people add skulls (5th skull loses); discarding red cards from your deck causes wounds that can kill tribe members (adding a skull). — Paleo — [GameRules: Paleo](https://gamerules.com/rules/paleo/)
- **Threat track**: bad rolls or terror cards raise a threat level that can kill hostages or end the conversation. — Hostage Negotiator — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/hostage-negotiator-review/)
- **Shared vehicle health**: ship enemies strike Serenity; losing the ship loses the game. — Legendary Encounters: Firefly — [Miniature Market review](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/legendary-encounters-firefly-deck-building-game-review)
- **Allied defenders with retaliation**: Dahan (2 health each) take ravage damage and surviving Dahan deal 2 damage back. — Spirit Island — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Cross-turn co-operation**: cards can be played on other players' turns (Coordinate); in Hogwarts Battle tokens saved from other heroes' turns can be spent on your own. — Legendary Encounters: Alien; Hogwarts Battle — [Order of Gamers](https://www.orderofgamers.com/downloads/LegendaryEncountersAlien_v1.1.pdf); [Rulespal: Hogwarts](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)

### Inferences
- "Spill-over" (Aeon's End exhausted players doubling damage to Gravehold) prevents player elimination while preserving consequence; directly analogous to staff burnout pushing harm onto Reputation.
- Junk cards come in three strengths: tempo tax (wounds/status), score tax (corruption), and lethal timer (chestburster). Thunderstone's "wounds shrink hand" is a junk-free alternative.

### Gaps
- Thunderstone Quest's competitive-default and Barricades co-op mode defence rules (barricade wall health) not read in detail ([Barricades rulebook](https://alderac.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Barricades_Rulebook.pdf) exists).
- LOTR DBG Archenemy and Two Towers "Wall" deck mechanics only listed, not explained ([Rulespal: Two Towers](https://rulespal.com/lotr-two-towers-deck-building/rulebook)).
- Resident Evil DBG has Story, Mercenary and Versus modes ([Reactor](https://reactormag.com/the-analog-gamer-deck-building-card-games-part-4-of-4-resident-evil-the-deck-building-game/)); no "mission mode" was found and the Mansion deck exploration rules were not read.

## 3. Progression: campaigns, legacy, XP, unlocks, scaling difficulty

### Takeaway
Progression splits into (a) **between-game character persistence** (stickers, XP, treasures) with decks reset each mission, (b) **content unlocks** (sealed boxes, new market cards), and (c) **opponent scaling** that mirrors player growth (upgraded nemesis basics, tiered campaign years). The best-regarded designs scale threat in step with rewards.

### Cited Findings
- **Sequential unlock boxes (campaign years)**: seven numbered game boxes, each opened after winning the previous, add components and increase difficulty. — Harry Potter: Hogwarts Battle — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook); [JB Hi-Fi listing](https://www.jbhifi.com.au/products/usaopoly-harry-potter-hogwarts-battle)
- **Build-your-own character (legacy)**: Aeon's End Legacy has no pre-made mages; players create their own, choose new character upgrades and add stronger player cards to the market after each game; sealed boxes A–D open at scripted points; finished mages remain playable with all Aeon's End content. — Aeon's End Legacy — [Punchboard review](https://punchboard.co.uk/review-aeons-end-legacy/); [Aeon's End Wiki: Legacy](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Legacy)
- **Expedition (mini-campaign with mutual escalation)**: winning battles adds random treasure cards of increasing level (5 L1, 3 L2, 5 L3, 5 L4) to a persistent Barracks, while the nemesis deck replaces basic cards with "Upgraded Basic" cards per tier (none in battle 1, up to 3/5/7 in battles 4–5). — Aeon's End (War Eternal onward, New Age, Outcasts) — [Aeon's End Wiki: Expedition](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Expedition); [Aeon's End Wiki: Outcasts](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Outcasts)
- **Karma stickers on character board**: missions award karma (3 on a win, 1 on a successful abort) spent on permanent upgrade stickers (cheapest 5 karma); decks reset to starters each mission; missions are graded (Crossfire → Extraction → Dragon Fight). Reviewers found upgrades too slow/expensive. — Shadowrun: Crossfire — [Board Game Quest](https://boardgamequest.com/shadowrun-crossfire-review); [Theology of Games](https://theologyofgames.com/blog/2015/3/22/we-got-stranded-a-double-take-review-of-shadowrun-crossfire)
- **XP-for-card-level deck upgrades (construction, not in-game buying)**: experience earned per scenario buys cards whose level equals their XP cost, replacing existing cards; upgrading to a higher version of the same card costs only the difference (minimum 1). — Arkham Horror: The Card Game — [FFG: That Which Doesn't Kill Us](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/en/news/2017/5/23/that-which-doesnt-kill-us/); [FFG forum archive](https://ffg-forum-archive.entropicdreams.com/topic/314219-spending-xp-on-card-upgrades)
- **Trauma (persistent penalty)**: permanent physical/mental trauma is recorded in the campaign log and starts each later scenario as pre-applied damage/horror. — Arkham Horror LCG — [FFG: Uncovering the Truth](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/en/news/2016/9/7/uncovering-the-truth/)
- **Campaign log with branching state**: outcomes are recorded and alter later scenarios; XP is earned even after defeat. — Arkham Horror LCG — [FFG](https://www.fantasyflightgames.com/en/news/2016/9/7/uncovering-the-truth/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkham_Horror:_The_Card_Game)
- **Balance patch list (Taboo)**: an official list changes XP costs of specific cards without changing their level. — Arkham Horror LCG — [FFG Taboo List PDF](https://images-cdn.fantasyflightgames.com/filer_public/01/88/0188164a-0688-4ca7-9740-2643e66966b7/taboo_list_21.pdf)
- **Legacy stickers, destruction and contracts**: permanent stickers on cards and board, torn-up components; completing contracts adds new cards to the adventure deck or upgrades cards/spaces; a 10+ game campaign that cannot be reset. — Clank! Legacy: Acquisitions Incorporated — [Comicbook.com review](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/clank-legacy-board-game-review); [Dire Wolf FAQ](https://direwolfdigital.com/clank-legacy-acquisitions-incorporated/faq). Sequel Darkest Magic adds scratch-off cards and character journals — [Tlama Games listing](https://www.tlamagames.com/en/board-games/clank--legacy-2--acquisitions-incorporated-darkest-magic/)
- **Between-episode currency**: completing objectives earns credits spent between episodes on crew healing, ship repairs or upgrades. — Legendary Encounters: Firefly — [Miniature Market review](https://www.miniaturemarket.com/reviewcorner/legendary-encounters-firefly-deck-building-game-review)
- **Hero levelling via XP**: heroes are levelled into higher heroes by spending XP (e.g. printed level + 2, usually 5 XP); a guild location lets an extra hero level up. — Thunderstone Quest — [Frozen in Time rulebook](https://alderac.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Q07_FROZEN-IN-TIME_Rulebook.pdf); [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/thunderstone-quest/rulebook)
- **Quest-pack scenario modularity**: Thunderstone Quest ships content as numbered Quests with their own rulebooks. — Thunderstone Quest — [What Lies Beneath rulebook](https://alderac.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Q06_WHAT-LIES-BENEATH_Rulebook.pdf)
- **In-game level up from deck flow**: in Mage Knight solo, after each action the player can level up (fame-based), and the conquest goal is two cities. — Mage Knight — [BoardGaming.com tips](https://boardgaming.com/gametips/mage-knight-board-game)
- **Campaign XP across mission tiers**: Hero Realms campaign decks award experience to progress through mission levels; one reviewer says a campaign deck raises a character by about one level. — Hero Realms — [Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2018/04/11/matt-carlson-hero-realms/)
- **Act structure with ascension and unlocks**: Slay the Spire board game has acts, a bonus fourth act/true ending, ascension difficulty levels and unlockable cards; boss nodes reward Boss Relics; combat rewards are gold, maybe a potion, and pick 1 of 3 cards. — Slay the Spire: The Board Game — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/board-games/the-slay-the-spire-board-game-might-be-a-little-too-faithful-to-the-videogame-but-its-co-op-mode-offers-a-whole-new-perspective-on-the-iconic-roguelike); [OfficialGameRules](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/slay-the-spire/)
- **Modular scenario decks**: Paleo assembles each game from scenario modules; reviewers note heavy card sorting at first. — Paleo — [Opinionated Gamers](https://opinionatedgamers.com/2020/10/23/dale-yu-first-impressions-of-paleo-spoiler-free/); [Tabletop Gaming](https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/reviews/paleo-review)
- **Spirit Island difficulty dial**: Adversary levels and Scenario difficulty ratings (0–10, +3 for thematic maps) are summed as an explicit challenge score. — Spirit Island — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

### Inferences
- **Arkham LCG vs. deck-builders**: Arkham's deck is fixed before play and changes only between scenarios via XP; true deck-builders grow decks during play and usually reset afterwards (Shadowrun, Aeon's End). A hybrid (Aeon's End Expedition, Firefly credits) keeps in-game building and adds a small persistent layer.
- Mirrored scaling (Aeon's End Expedition: treasures for players, upgraded basics for the nemesis) is the cleanest documented answer to "progression makes the game too easy".
- Shadowrun reviewers' complaint about slow karma suggests reward pacing should yield a visible upgrade every 1–2 sessions.

### Gaps
- Hogwarts Battle per-year content (proficiencies in Game 6, Horcruxes in Game 7, dice in Game 3+) — [unverified]; the fetched rulebook summary did not describe Games 2–7.
- Clank! Legacy's specific persistent elements (character "titles", company upgrades, dragon/boss changes) not detailed.
- Arkham LCG death/insanity removing the investigator and their XP comes only from [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TabletopGame/ArkhamHorrorTheCardGame) — informal.

## 4. Deck-flow mechanics: no-shuffle decks, prepped spells, recharge, hand-building

### Takeaway
Several co-op designs alter the basic draw–play–discard–shuffle loop to give planning: Aeon's End never shuffles and makes discard order a puzzle; spells are "prepped" a turn ahead; Spirit Island uses reclaim instead of reshuffle; Hostage Negotiator buys straight into hand.

### Cited Findings
- **Ordered discard (no shuffle)**: when the deck runs out the discard pile is flipped as-is to form the new deck; players may view but not rearrange the discard; hand is kept between turns with no hand limit. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Prep-then-cast**: spells are played to a breach on one turn and cast at the start of the next; spells on open breaches may be held, on closed breaches must be cast; a cast spell goes to discard before resolving. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Upgradable slots (breaches)**: each mage has breaches I–IV that start closed; paying a focus cost rotates a breach and lowers its open cost; opened breaches stay open and may grant bonuses. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Charge meter / ultimate**: pay 2 aether per charge; when the mage's 4–6 charge spaces fill, the unique ability can be used, spending all charges. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Fixed-pile market**: nine supply piles chosen randomly/by players/by guideline (3 gems, 2 relics, 4 spells), not refilled when empty; Legacy requires minimum counts per type. — Aeon's End — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules)
- **Use-it-or-lose-it currency**: unspent aether is lost at end of turn (Aeon's End); unused attack/influence tokens are discarded (Hogwarts Battle). — [Aeon's End Wiki](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Rules); [Rulespal: Hogwarts](https://rulespal.com/harry-potter-hogwarts-battle/rulebook)
- **Hand-building (buy to hand)**: purchased conversation cards go straight into the next turn's hand rather than cycling through a deck. — Hostage Negotiator — [Zatu review](https://zatu.com/hostage-negotiator-review/)
- **Health-as-hand-size**: draw equals current HP. — Thunderstone Quest — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/thunderstone-quest/rulebook)
- **Reclaim instead of reshuffle**: played power cards sit in a discard until a Reclaim growth option returns all of them (or a "Reclaim One" returns one). — Spirit Island — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Off-turn play**: Coordinate cards can be played on another player's turn and the player draws a replacement. — Legendary Encounters — [Order of Gamers](https://www.orderofgamers.com/downloads/LegendaryEncountersAlien_v1.1.pdf)

### Inferences
- No-shuffle + prep gives the planning depth that co-op needs to counter a visible threat; it suits a game where players respond to telegraphed patient deterioration.

### Gaps
- Mage Knight's own deck mechanics (powered vs basic card effects via mana, wounds into deck, sideways play for 1) — [unverified], background knowledge.

## 5. Spirit Island specifics

### Takeaway
Spirit Island is a deck-builder without a shuffled deck: hand size is the full owned card pool, gated by **card plays** and **energy** that grow as **presence** leaves two tracks; **elements** from played cards unlock innate powers; new cards come from a **draw-4-keep-1** draft of Minor or Major powers; **fear** is the players' win engine.

### Cited Findings
- **Phase structure**: Spirit → Fast Power → Invader → Slow Power → Time Passes, all simultaneous. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Growth choice**: each spirit picks one growth option (add presence, gain energy, gain a power card, reclaim). — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Presence tracks reveal economy**: removing presence from the Energy and Card Plays tracks uncovers higher numbers (only the highest counts) and sometimes bonus elements. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Banked energy**: energy carries over between turns but unused card plays do not; cost and elements are paid at play, effects resolve in the matching phase. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Fast/slow timing**: fast powers resolve before invaders act, slow powers after. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Element thresholds (checked, not spent)**: eight elements from cards in play this turn are counted against thresholds on innate powers and some cards. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Draft of four**: choose Minor or Major deck, draw 4, keep 1; taking a Major forces you to Forget (permanently remove) one owned power card. Power Progression lists give a fixed order for beginners. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Fear pool → fear cards**: fear from powers and from destroying towns (1) and cities (2) fills a pool; each full pool earns a fear card resolved next invader phase; terror-level dividers progressively relax the victory condition (no invaders → no towns/cities → no cities). — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- **Invader phase order**: Blighted Island effect → fear cards → Ravage → Build → Explore → advance cards; build only in lands with invaders. — [Rulespal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook); [OfficialGameRules](https://officialgamerules.org/game-rules/spirit-island/)

### Inferences
- The fear system is a "player-side escalation deck": progress accumulates into cards that weaken the opponent and lower the bar for victory, a mirror of threat escalation that a hospital game could use (e.g. "ward control" making cure targets easier).
- Forget-on-Major is an elegant deck-thinning cost that keeps power spikes from bloating decks.

### Gaps
- Exact growth options per spirit, Event deck (Branch & Claw), and Adversary level effects not researched.
